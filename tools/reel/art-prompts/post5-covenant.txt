## why this one
It took two of the three picks, is the only concept native to the house style (a red-chalk forearm-and-grip study is literally Leonardo's output, so the surface comes for free and there is no face or pose for sentiment to attach to), and the one judge who scored it low flagged nothing fatal — every objection he raised was prompt-level and he named the fix himself. Kneeling-before-dawn scored higher on truth-and-craft but its weaknesses are concept-level rather than fixable: both other judges said a bearded man kneeling in a dark room sits in the densest kitsch basin in the corpus and gets pattern-matched and scrolled past even when it renders perfectly, which is the one outcome this brief cannot afford two posts after the furnace.

## check the returned image for
Grafts applied, so check these first: (1) HANDS — one hand only, five fingers, no second hand, no third arm, no duplicated or fused forearm. I collapsed the two-hand overlapping grip to a single fist with the rope taking one turn around the bare forearm precisely because overlapping grips are the most reliably mangled subject in generation; if a second hand or a phantom arm appears, reroll rather than retouch. Check the rope turn reads as rope over skin and has not fused into the wrist. (2) NO HORIZONTAL — confirm the well parapet did not come back as a lit band crossing the upper quarter, and that rope-plus-anything has not resolved into an accidental cross. The stone must be a dark silhouette confined to the top-left corner with the worn groove visible; if it spans the frame, reroll. (3) BORDER — the known prior failure. No painted border, no decorative edging, no torn or curled parchment edge, no visible sheet of paper inside the picture, no mat, no vignette. Drawing must run off all four edges. (4) SCALE — the other known prior failure. The fist and forearm should feel close enough to touch and the lit grip should sit between roughly 25% and 48% of the frame height; if it reads as a small still life floating in a large empty sheet, reroll. (5) BOTTOM THIRD — draw a line at 55% height and confirm everything below it is plain dark umber with only the rope passing through: no knuckle, no groove, no stone, no detail that the quote scrim would amputate. (6) TEXT — zero letters, numbers, script or signature anywhere, and specifically nothing carved or inked on the stone, which is where generators like to put a mark. No Hebrew. (7) PALETTE — strictly umber, ochre and sanguine; reject any grey, blue or green cast in the paper, any digital gloss, airbrushed or photoreal skin, or a glowing light source, beam, lamp or flame. (8) SURFACE — it should look like a chalk-and-ink study sheet with sparse hatching in the open upper background, not a finished illustrated genre scene of a well. (9) THUMBNAIL TEST — shrink to about 200px and squint: you should see one bright diagonal against a dark field and read "a hand holding on under load" inside a second. If it reads as nautical rigging, climbing gear or a gym rope rather than strain, or if the lit values scatter, reroll. (10) Confirm no bucket, load, chain, pulley, glove or modern hardware crept in, and no face or second figure anywhere.
