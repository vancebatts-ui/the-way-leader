# Chapter 5 — The Cost of the Covenant — "the-daily-hold", TZITZIT VERSION
# USED 2026-11-13 in Canva Dream Lab, 9:16, Canva Craft 1. 1493 chars (cap is 1500).
#
# WHY THIS REPLACED THE ROPE VERSION
# The rope cut (post5-covenant-USED.txt) rendered a dark brown forearm with hemp rope coiled
# twice around the wrist. Intent was a working hold — endurance, holding on under load. But a
# rope coiled around a Black wrist carries a bondage/captivity reading the author never asked
# for, and his words on it: "the people who want to see it in a negative light will, just to
# discredit us or cause confusion and apprehension." Vance proposed tzitzit instead.
#
# It is the better image on the merits, not merely the safer one. The rope was a METAPHOR for
# holding on; the tzitzit is the ACTUAL PRACTICE of holding on — Numbers 15:39, you look at it
# and you remember and you do. That is exactly what the quote asks ("the faithfulness you
# maintain on hard ones"), and it now rhymes with the chapter title.
#
# THREE DELIBERATE CONSTRAINTS, DO NOT DROP THEM ON A REROLL
# 1. Knots read as TEXTURE AND SHADOW, never as crisp countable detail. Winding counts are
#    rule-governed and his audience includes people who will count them; generators mangle this
#    exactly the way they mangle Hebrew. Suggest, never state.
# 2. NO BLUE THREAD. Techelet is a live dispute and a picture should not make a ruling. It also
#    breaks the umber/ochre palette. A chalk study is monochrome by medium, so its absence says
#    nothing either way — which is the point.
# 3. PLAIN UNDYED WORKING GARMENT, not a striped prayer shawl. The quote is about hard ordinary
#    days, not liturgy. A tallit at worship turns it into a prayer scene and reads as stock
#    Judaica.
#
# Also note the explicit negative "Nothing is wrapped, coiled, tied or bound around the wrist or
# arm" — without it the model drifts back toward the rope wrap.
#
# OPEN QUESTION PUT TO HIM, built on the assumption that the answer is yes: tzitzit on the figure
# makes a claim about WHO is wearing it, which is more particular than the rope was. Assumed to
# be a feature given the Hebraic-restoration thrust of the book. He has not corrected it.

Sanguine chalk and brown-ink study after Leonardo on aged parchment: cross-hatching, umber wash, deep chiaroscuro. A close study of ONE weathered work-hardened hand and forearm, skin deep dark brown, a Black African labourer's arm, emphatically not European and not pale. The hand is closed around the knotted fringe at the corner of a plain undyed coarse wool garment, lifting it; the twisted cords spill through the fist and fall away down the frame. Knuckles raised, tendons taut, cloth bunched in the grip. The forearm enters from the lower left, the fist high in frame between a quarter and half its height, one strong silhouette. The knots and windings read as soft texture and shadow, never crisp countable detail. Exactly one hand and one forearm: no second hand or arm, no body, no face, five fingers, no fused digits. Nothing is wrapped, coiled, tied or bound around the wrist or arm. No rope, no chain, no cord encircling the skin. Hard raking light from upper left, no visible source. Upper background bare parchment with sparse hatching. Below 55% height: plain umber shadow, only the falling cords, no detail. Umber, ochre and sanguine only; no blue thread, no grey or green cast, no gloss, no photorealism. Vertical 9:16, full bleed: no frame, no border, no margin, no vignette, no paper edge. No horizontal band, no cross, no halo, no beam, no flame. Absolutely no text, letters, numbers, Hebrew or any script, no writing or embroidery on the cloth, no signature, no watermark.
