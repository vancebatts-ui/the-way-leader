## why this one
Both runners-up carry a fatal flaw on a criterion that outranks feed mechanics: the helmet is irreducibly Ephesians 6 to this audience, so nest-in-the-helmet argues that the man's salvation has been colonized and removed, which the chapter does not claim, and crouching-at-the-door depends on a shadow that a generator will either resolve into demon-dog kitsch or into nothing at all. Voice-at-his-shoulder is the only concept that is natively a da Vinci subject, makes no claim that can be doctrinally wrong, and whose two weaknesses — the worn hand-at-temple gesture and the thumbnail blur — were the same weakness, fixed by one change that two independent judges converged on: close the hand at the jaw, light the knuckle plane hard, and let it read as a bright separated mass at 200px the way the nest was supposed to.

## check the returned image for
1) THE HAND, first and hardest: count the fingers and the knuckles. Four fingers, one thumb along the jawline, fingers together and closed — if they are splayed, fused, doubled, six-count, or the hand is flattened over the ear like "hear no evil," reroll, do not retouch. The back of the hand must read as one lit plane, not a tangle. 2) THE HEAD TURN: is the head genuinely turned off axis with the eyes leaving the frame past his own shoulder, or has the model regressed to a frontal, symmetrical, calm head looking at the viewer? A frontal head inverts the concept from interrogation into reverie — reroll. 3) THE BACKGROUND: scan the dark behind and past his shoulder at full size AND at 200px for anything resolving — a second face, a hooded shape, a hand, smoke, eyes, a creature, a wall edge. One shape in the shadow turns this into the horned-devil allegory the brief forbids. 4) THE EXPRESSION: sober and hard, or pained? Check for furrowed/knotted brow, squeezed eyes, open or downturned mouth, tilted-down head, tears. A man with a headache or a man in anguish is a weak man and fails the house style. 5) BORDER: look at all four edges for a painted border, a drawn rectangle, a tonal inset panel, a soft vignette box or an empty parchment margin. This killed a previous take — check it before anything else aesthetic. 6) SCALE: does the figure fill most of the height, or has the model shrunk him and left ground around him? Shrinking the figure was the other previous failure. 7) THE SCRIM ZONE: cover the bottom 45% with your thumb. Nothing essential may be under there — no part of the face, no part of the raised hand, no second point of interest. Eyes should sit roughly a third down from the top. 8) LETTERING: sweep the whole sheet, especially lower corners and any lit edge, for stray marks that resolve as letters, numerals, a monogram or a signature flourish. 9) THUMBNAIL TEST: shrink to 200px. Are brow, cheekbone and knuckles still three readable bright shapes against dark, or have beard, hair and hand merged into one umber blob? If the gesture is gone, it is just a bearded portrait and it fails beside the furnace post. 10) PALETTE AND SURFACE: warm umber, ochre, sanguine and cream only — no blue, green or cool grey anywhere, no specular shine, no airbrush smoothness, no 3D or photographic look, and visible chalk grain and laid-paper texture present rather than a slick finished painting. 11) WARDROBE: plain coarse linen, throat open, nothing anachronistic or ornamental, no armour, no amulet, no earpiece.
